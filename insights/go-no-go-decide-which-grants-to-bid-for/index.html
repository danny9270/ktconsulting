Go or no-go? How to decide which grants are worth bidding for

Kelly Tsanova30 October 2026 · 6 min read

When I was Program Development Manager at Hagar International in Vietnam, our grant submission rate reached 91% and our award rate 55%. People tend to assume a number like that comes down to the writing. Good writing matters, but in my experience the bigger factor is choosing well what to go for in the first place.

Every proposal takes days or weeks of time from programme, finance and partner staff, people who are usually busy running programmes too. A go/no-go decision, made early and honestly, protects that time for the bids you can actually win.

Ask these questions before anyone writes a word

1. Are we eligible, without stretching?

Check legal status and registration, the countries covered, the minimum and maximum grant size, any requirement for a local or international partner and any co-financing you'd need to find. If you have to bend the rules to fit, the answer is almost always no.

2. Does it fund what we already do, or plan to do?

The strongest bids come from work you believe in and know how to deliver. If you find yourself redesigning your programme to match the call, stop and ask whether you'd still want to run it if the money weren't there. Donors can tell when an organisation is chasing funding.

3. Do we know this donor, and do they know us?

Have you met the donor's staff, read their strategy and spoken to organisations they've funded before? Have you delivered work of a similar size? An open call to an organisation the donor has never heard of is a long shot. That doesn't always mean no, but it should lower your expectations, and it's a sign to start building the relationship for next time.

4. Do we have the people and time to write it well?

Count back from the deadline. Who will write the narrative, build the budget and gather partner documents? A rushed proposal written by someone squeezing it in between other jobs rarely wins. If the time isn't there, you either find support or let the call go.

5. Could we manage the grant if we won?

This is the question teams most often skip. Look at the reporting requirements, the audit rules, whether funding is paid in advance or reimbursed afterwards and what that means for your cash flow. Bring finance into the decision from the start. Winning a grant you can't manage safely is worse than not winning it.

6. What's the strategic value, even if we lose?

Sometimes a bid is worth it because it opens a door to a new donor, strengthens a partnership or helps your team develop an idea you'll use again. Be honest about whether that's really true, or a way of justifying a bid you already wanted to make.

Make it simple and make it quick

You don't need a complicated tool. Score each question from 1 to 3, agree beforehand which answers are automatic deal-breakers, and decide as a team within a few days of the call coming out. Write down the reasons, whichever way you go. Over a year or two those notes will show you which kinds of calls you win and which you don't.

Saying no is a decision too. Each well-made "no" frees your team to do a better job on the bids that matter. A smaller pipeline of strong proposals beats a long list of rushed ones.
